title: "the internet is fascinating"
date: 2026-09-22

right now, somewhere under an ocean, a fucking laser is probably carrying the data for this blog post through a strand of glass. same goes for your data.

and you're probably using that data to watch youtube, open instagram, ssh into some random vps, or watch a roblox kid run around shooting people.

actual physical data is moving through glass thousands of kilometers away from you because you clicked something on a computer.

that's fucking fascinating.

i've been thinking about the internet lately and honestly it's kinda weird once you stop treating it as this magical abstract thing called "the internet". it's literally just a bunch of computers connected to other computers.

take some random kid playing roblox. he presses a key, his computer sends some data, and that data physically travels through cables, fiber, networking equipment, whatever, until it reaches some server somewhere. the server processes it, sends data back, and now his roblox character does whatever the fuck he told it to do.

and somewhere in that chain, there might literally be packets travelling through a glass fiber cable under an ocean. just so some random kid can play roblox.

the more you think about it, the weirder it gets.

you can almost imagine it as one insanely long cable connecting your computer to theirs. you rent a connection from an isp, plug in over ethernet or wi-fi, and on the other end their machine is plugged into a network too.

obviously that's not how it actually works. there are routers everywhere, different networks, isps connecting to other isps, peering points, data centers, submarine cables, switches, wireless links. if you've ever run traceroute and counted the hops, you know the path between two machines can be a stupid number of individual steps.

but the mental model still holds, roughly. your computer is physically connected to a network. that network is connected to another network. eventually, after a bunch of machines and physical infrastructure, you reach another computer. and all of it happens so fast that from your perspective the other computer just feels... there.

you type a url, press enter, and a website appears. no magic. just computers exchanging data.

sure, there are a ridiculous number of layers involved. dns, ip, tcp, udp, http, tls, ethernet, routing protocols, fiber, radio, electrical signals. but underneath all of those abstractions, something physical is happening. bits have to actually go somewhere. they don't disappear into "the cloud".

the cloud isn't a fucking cloud. it's computers sitting in buildings, connected to networks, connected to other networks.

i think that's what makes it so fascinating to me. we've built so many layers on top of this physical infrastructure that we barely think about the physical part anymore. you open github and think you're opening github. you ssh into a vps and think you're connecting to a vps. you send a discord message and it just appears on the other person's screen.

and then there's the scale. some of the data you send crosses thousands of kilometers, through fiber under an entire fucking ocean. all because you wanted to load a webpage. or download a package. or send "bro look at this" to your friend. or make your roblox character move.

it's absurd when you stop and think about it. technology like this feels like something that shouldn't exist. not literally, obviously it doesn't violate physics. it's more like... how the fuck did humans build something this complicated and then make it so normal that we just go "my wi-fi is slow today."

😭

strip away the websites, apps, apis, and cloud platforms. at the bottom it's still just machines exchanging data through physical infrastructure. cables. routers. fiber. packets. computers.

and somehow all of that became "the internet".

crazy times we live in.
